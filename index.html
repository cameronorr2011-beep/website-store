Dosing

Salt and f/2, at every size

Salt makes the water; f/2 feeds what grows in it. Both are dosed by volume, and the two pack sizes we sell are built around the same arithmetic.

Our f/2 is a 500× single-bottle concentrate, dosed at 2 mL per litre. Salt is 30 g per litre (30 ppt). Round to the nearest gram or half millilitre — the culture will not notice.

  • Dose the f/2 into new saltwater, before the culture. Add it, swirl, then pour the culture in. At every split, dose only the fresh water you added — a split with no new f/2 is a dilution, not a feeding.
  • Never dose f/2 into a tank with animals in it. It is algae food. In a larval or reef tank it feeds bacteria and unwanted algae, not the animal you are trying to feed.
  • Holding a strain rather than growing volume? Halve it: 1 mL per litre keeps a backup alive at a slower division rate without over-shooting the nutrient.
  • The bag is bigger than the kit. A gallon needs 114 g, so 1,000 g covers the first batch and about seven more splits. At 30 g per litre it makes roughly 33 litres.

Strain detail · green

Nannochloropsis oculata

The hardiest bottle we sell, and the food behind the food. This is what you grow to feed rotifers, brine shrimp and green water — not a larval diet on its own.

Cell size
2–3 µm
Type
Green, round, non-motile
Salt
30 g per litre
Light
12–16 h a day
Split
Weekly, about 1:1
DHA
Not detected

Best use

  • Rotifer cultures. The standard green feed for Brachionus in marine hatcheries. Rotifers crop it readily and take up its EPA. It carries no DHA, so if the next step is a bivalve or fish larva, give the rotifers a T-Iso feed before they go out.
  • Brine shrimp and copepods. The same job at a smaller scale: a 2–3 µm cell that stays in the water column long enough to be filtered.
  • Green water. A live tint for larval and grow-out tanks that feeds the zooplankton in the water rather than the animal directly.
  • A first culture. Dense growth on f/2, a wide temperature range, and it survives a missed split better than T-Iso does.

Where it is the wrong pick

As the only feed for bivalve larvae. In a 21-day trial, Pacific oyster larvae fed N. oculata alone did not grow, and the authors could not detect DHA in the cells. A later trial with four bivalve species found the larvae preferentially rejected N. oculata and grew worse on it than on a diatom or Isochrysis. Feed it to the rotifers; put T-Iso or a blend in the larval tank.

How to grow it

30 g of salt per litre (30 ppt), 20–24 °C, 12 to 16 hours of light and a gentle bubble. Dose f/2 at 2 mL per litre of new water, and split about half every week into fresh saltwater dosed at the same rate. Keep one vessel back and you will not need to buy the strain again.

What this is based on

  1. Sakamoto, Okimasu & Amemura (1997), Aquaculture Science 45(4): 527–531 — oyster larvae fed N. oculata alone did not grow; no DHA detected. doi
  2. Liao et al. (2017), PLOS ONE 12(6): e0180730 — four bivalve species rejected N. oculata and grew worse on it. doi
  3. Osmanoğlu, Elp & Durmaz (2024), Journal of Applied Phycology 36: 2655–2663 — Nannochloropsis lacks DHA, so rotifers need a DHA source before they are fed out. doi

Tisochrysis lutea (T-Iso)

The DHA half of the ration, and the strain most oyster hatcheries build a larval feed around.

Cell size
3–7 µm
Type
Golden, swimming (flagellate)
Salt
30 g per litre
Light
12–16 h a day
Split
Weekly, about 1:1
DHA
High — with EPA

Best use

  • Bivalve larvae. Oyster, clam, mussel and scallop larvae from first feed: 3–7 µm, rich in DHA, and flagellated, so it stays in the water column where the larvae are instead of settling out.
  • Spat and juveniles. The same cell carries through after set, alone or with a small diatom.
  • Rotifer enrichment. A second feed for rotifers that will go to bivalve or fish larvae. Nannochloropsis supplies EPA but no DHA; this supplies the DHA.
  • With a diatom. FAO's hatchery manual is direct: a mixed diet of two or three species, including a suitably sized diatom and a flagellate, beats any single species. T-Iso is the flagellate in that pair.

Where it is the wrong pick

As a complete diet on its own. Larvae can make some EPA from precursors, but mixed diets still grow faster and survive better — that is what the larval blend is for. If you are raising larvae to sell, pair it with a small diatom rather than running it alone.

How to grow it

20–24 °C, 30 g of salt per litre, 12 to 16 hours of light, and air that moves the water without churning it. Dose f/2 at 2 mL per litre of new water. It divides faster than Nannochloropsis. Watch the colour: rich gold is healthy, pale straw means it has run out of food.

What this is based on

  1. Helm, Bourne & Lovatelli (2004), FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 471 — mixed diets including a diatom and a flagellate outperform single species. fao.org
  2. Liu et al. (2016), Aquaculture 452: 326–341 — best larval growth from T-Iso plus Chaetoceros. doi
  3. da Costa et al. (2015), Lipids — larvae fed T-Iso alone had to synthesise EPA from precursors. PubMed
