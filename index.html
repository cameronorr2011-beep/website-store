Strain detail · green

Nannochloropsis oculata

The smallest cell we sell and the easiest to keep alive. If you have never grown algae, this is the bottle to start with.

Feeds
Rotifers, brine shrimp, copepods

What it is

A round green cell with a thick wall. It grows dense in a gallon with f/2, handles a wider temperature range than the other five strains, and survives a missed split better than any of them.

How to grow it

30 parts per thousand saltwater, 20–24 °C, 12 to 16 hours of light and a gentle bubble. Split it about half every week into fresh saltwater dosed with f/2 at 2 mL per litre. Keep one vessel back and you will not need to buy the strain again.

Where it is the wrong pick

As the only feed for oyster larvae. The thick wall makes it hard to digest, so larvae fed only this strain grow slowly. Feed it to the rotifers and brine shrimp that feed the larvae, and put T-Iso or a larval blend in the tank.

Strain detail · golden-brown

Isochrysis aff. galbana (T-Iso)

The base feed in most oyster hatcheries. Golden-brown, rich in DHA, and fast to divide.

Cell size
4–6 µm
Type
Swimming golden cell
Salt
30 ppt
Light
12–16 h a day
Split
Weekly, about 1:1
Feeds
Oyster, clam and mussel larvae from first feed

What it is

Two whip-like tails, a golden-brown colour and a habit of staying up in the water column. Because it swims, it does not settle out the way a diatom does, so gentle air is enough to keep it in suspension.

How to grow it

20–24 °C, 30 ppt, 12 to 16 hours of light, and air that moves the water without churning it. It divides faster than Nannochloropsis. Watch the colour: rich gold is healthy, pale straw means it has run out of food.

Where it is the wrong pick

As the only feed after set. It is small, and juveniles do better with a diatom in the mix — that is why the larval blend carries three strains rather than one. It is also the strain most easily damaged by heavy air, so do not treat it like a diatom culture.
